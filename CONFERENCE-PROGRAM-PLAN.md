# SIC 2027 — Three-Hall Scientific Program Plan

**Hyderabad Sports Injuries Conclave · 29th & 30th January 2027**
Draft for the Scientific Committee · prepared from a review of IASCON 2025/26, IASMCON 2025, ISKSAA, ISAKOS 2025, AOSSM 2025, ESSKA 2026, IFSPT/World Physiotherapy, NSCA, and the IOC/WADA/ACSM consensus literature. Every source is listed at the end of each hall section.

---

## 1. The architecture — why three halls

One hall forces a compromise programme: surgeons sit through nutrition talks, physiotherapists sit through arthroscopic technique. Three parallel halls let each profession get two full days of depth, while shared plenary sessions keep the conclave feeling like one event rather than three co-located meetings.

| | Hall A | Hall B | Hall C |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Title** | Surgery & Sports Orthopaedics | Rehabilitation, Performance & Return to Play | Sports Science, Nutrition & Athlete Health |
| **Audience** | Orthopaedic & arthroscopy surgeons, sports physicians, ortho PGs | Sports physiotherapists, S&C coaches, athletic trainers, physio PGs | Sports nutritionists, exercise physiologists, team doctors, psychologists, coaches |
| **Expected share** | ~45% | ~35% | ~20% |
| **Room need** | Largest (also hosts plenary) | Medium + floor space for hands-on | Smallest |

**Sizing rule:** Hall A doubles as the plenary hall, so it must seat the full delegate count. Halls B and C need roughly 40% and 25% of that.

### Joint plenary — everyone in one room

These are deliberately **not** parallel. They are the moments that make it one conclave:

| Session | When | Why joint |
|---|---|---|
| Inauguration | Day 1, 09:00 | Ceremonial; sets the theme |
| **Opening keynote** | Day 1, 09:15 | One big name, everyone hears it |
| **The Return-to-Play panel** — surgeon + physio + S&C + nutritionist + athlete | Day 1, close of morning | The single decision every profession in the building shares. This is the signature session of SIC 2027. |
| **Medical ethics & good clinical practice** | Day 1, late afternoon | **Mandatory for TSMC CPD accreditation** — the Declaration Proforma requires it. Running it jointly guarantees attendance is captured. |
| Athlete fireside chat | Day 2, midday | Draws every audience; strong media moment |
| Awards & valedictory | Day 2, close | Best paper/poster across all three tracks |

Everything else runs in parallel.

## 3. Hall A — Surgery & Sports Orthopaedics

**Format:** 12-minute talks (10 speak + 2 changeover), hard stops enforced by chairs. Debates 2 × 8 min + rebuttal with audience polling before and after. **Pre-record all "live" surgery** — the operating surgeon narrates the recording live ("relive"), which gives the teaching value without OT-link risk.

**A1 · Instability: beyond the Bankart** — measuring bone loss that changes the plan (3D-CT vs glenoid track vs MRI); Bankart + remplissage into subcritical bone loss; Latarjet 2027 (classic vs congruent-arc, screw vs button, open vs arthroscopic); free bone block after failed Latarjet; posterior and multidirectional instability in throwers and gymnasts; **relive:** arthroscopic Latarjet — the six steps where it goes wrong.
**Debate:** *"19-year-old kabaddi player, first dislocation — stabilise now vs rehab and wait."*

**A2 · Rotator cuff** — massive but reparable; SCR vs lower trapezius transfer (fatty infiltration as the discriminator); patch and bioinductive augmentation — what 2025 evidence supports; the subscapularis tear you're missing; case panel: three cuffs, three plans.

**A4 · ACL I** — graft selection 2027: is quadriceps tendon now default? (international survey: QT preferred for 61% primary, 89% revision); hamstring sizing and fixation; ACL repair with internal bracing — which 5%?; ramp lesions and why they're missed; **relive:** QT ACLR + modified Lemaire LET.
**Debate:** *"Every high-risk primary ACL needs a lateral extra-articular tenodesis."*

**A5 · ACL II** — posterior tibial slope and slope-reducing osteotomy in the re-ruptured knee; the multiligament knee in India (road traffic vs kabaddi patterns); PCL; posterolateral corner; RTS criteria when you have no isokinetic dynamometer.

**A6 · Save the meniscus** — posterior root tears and persistent extrusion; centralisation — promising or over-sold?; radial and horizontal cleavage tears; "meniscectomy is not a crime" — the 15% where resection is right; allograft/scaffold pathways in India.

**A7 · Cartilage & preservation** — the 2 cm² decision (microfracture vs AMIC vs minced vs MACI, with Indian cost reality); single-stage minced cartilage; osteochondral auto/allograft and the supply problem; unloading osteotomy as treatment; RTS after cartilage surgery — 9 months is a floor, not a target.

**A8 · Hip & groin** — groin pain algorithm for footballers and kabaddi players; FAI after the physiotherapy trials — who still gets a scope?; labral repair vs reconstruction vs augmentation; **how I do it:** hip arthroscopy setup and avoiding cam under-resection.
**Debate:** *borderline dysplasia (LCEA 20–25°) — arthroscopy + capsular plication vs PAO.*

**A9 · Foot & ankle** — chronic lateral instability decision tree; OLT size thresholds; Achilles in the athlete; syndesmosis (button vs screw, malreduction); posterior impingement in dancers.

**A10 · Paediatric & adolescent** — ACL in the skeletally immature (transphyseal vs all-epiphyseal vs IT-band; growth disturbance <2%, re-tear 9–15%); tibial spine avulsions; adolescent patellar instability — MPFL alone or address trochlea/TT-TG?; OCD; the overtrained 14-year-old.

**A11 · Orthobiologics** — what ESSKA-ORBIT supports (PRP in KL 1–3 knee OA, lateral epicondylitis) and what it doesn't; BMAC, adipose and "stem cell" clinics vs placebo-controlled data plus the Indian regulatory position; consent, cost and honest claims.
**Debate:** *"Biologics have no place in the arthroscopic OR in 2027."*

**A12 · Revision & failed surgery** *(reserve)* — finding the real cause of the failed ACL; one- vs two-stage revision; recurrent instability after Bankart/Latarjet; septic arthritis — retain or remove the graft?; **panel:** "My worst complication" — four faculty, unedited. *(Programme this last on Day 2 — it's the session people stay for.)*

**Sources:** [IASCON 2025 programme](https://iascon2025goa.com/download.html) · [IASCON 2026](https://iascon2026.com/) · [ISAKOS 2025](https://isakos.com/2025/Program) · [AOSSM 2025](https://events.sportsmed.org/am2025/full-program) · [ESSKA 2026](https://esska-congress.org/programme/program-overview/) · [ISKSAA](https://www.isksaa.com/) · [LET international consensus, Arthroscopy 2025](https://www.arthroscopyjournal.org/article/S0749-8063(25)00435-9/abstract) · [Quadriceps tendon global survey, JEO 2026](https://esskajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jeo2.70691) · [Ramp/root/radial current concepts, JASSM 2025](https://jassm.org/current-concepts-on-the-surgical-treatment-of-ramp-root-and-radial-meniscal-tears/) · [Minced cartilage vs ACI, KSSTA](https://esskajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ksa.70210) · [ESSKA-ORBIT PRP consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38436492/) · [PAO vs hip arthroscopy meta-analysis 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12221243/) · [Kabaddi knee injury epidemiology](https://brieflands.com/journals/asjsm/articles/13314)

## 4. Hall B — Rehabilitation, Performance & Return to Play

**Format:** 12–15 min talks; blocks close with a moderated discussion rather than per-talk Q&A. Two ticketed, capped hands-on workshops.

**B1 · Criteria-based rehab & the RTP decision** — "time is not a criterion"; what proportion of athletes would actually pass a full battery at 9 months post-ACLR; return to participation → sport → performance; psychological readiness (ACL-RSI) and the athlete whose knee passes but whose head doesn't; who owns the decision and how it's documented. Closes by building a one-page RTP criteria sheet live from the floor.
**Debate:** *"No athlete should be cleared for pivoting sport before 9 months post-ACLR."*

**B2 · Objective testing, screening & technology** — force plates in a small clinic: the five CMJ metrics worth reporting and the twenty that aren't; handheld dynamometry done properly (belt fixation, normalisation, MDC); isokinetics vs field testing in 2027; movement screening after FMS; markerless motion capture — good enough to change a decision?; **panel:** *"What would you buy first with ₹5 lakh?"*

**B3 · Tendinopathy & load-driven pain** — tendon pain as a loading problem; isometrics for analgesia (where "45 seconds × 5" came from and when it fails); heavy slow resistance vs eccentrics for patellar tendinopathy; insertional vs mid-portion Achilles; gluteal tendinopathy — compression is the enemy; energy storage and release, the phase everyone skips.

**W1 · Hands-on carousel** (90 min, three 28-min stations, ~30 per station):
1. **Objective testing bench** — live CMJ/drop jump on volunteers, reading force–time curves on screen; dynamometry with vs without belt fixation (showing the tester-strength artefact); writing the actual report line a surgeon and a coach will each read.
2. **Tendon loading lab** — Spanish squat rigged with a belt and pole (no proprietary kit); HSR tempo work; modifying every exercise for a clinic with one bench, one step, one barbell; the 24-hour pain-response rule on case vignettes.
3. **Plyometric progression** — pogo → hop → bound ladder with landing-quality criteria; take a 3-month post-ACLR athlete to month 7 with only cones, a box and floor space.

**W2 · Field-based RTP testing & hamstring reconditioning** (Day 2, 90 min) — build a 40-minute RTP battery with no lab (505, hop series, Askling H-test, sprint exposure); hamstring L-protocol and Nordic technique coached hands-on; sprint reintroduction dosing; the pass/fail sheet you hand the coach.

**B5 · Muscle injury & the hamstring** — classifying injury (BAMIC, MLG-R) and whether MRI changes Monday; L-protocol vs conventional (28 vs 51 days) and reinjury; lengthened-state training and adherence reality; calf and adductor — the two you're under-rehabbing; return to running → return to sprinting.

**B6 · Load management & injury risk** — what happened to ACWR (from "predicts injury" to a cluster RCT in 482 elite youth footballers showing no difference); metrics that survive a real Indian club season; bowling workload and the fast bowler's back; sleep, energy availability and travel — the load that never appears in the GPS file.

**B7 · The athlete who isn't a 25-year-old male** — youth resistance training is protective (and how to say that to a parent); apophyseal injury — load modification, not rest; the female athlete and menstrual cycle: what low-quality evidence permits you to claim; REDs as a modifiable rehab variable; para-athlete rehab and why your normative data doesn't apply.

**B4 · Adjuncts under scrutiny** *(reserve)* — BFR: what 2024–25 meta-analyses actually show (comparable, not superior) and the three groups where the cuff earns its place; manual therapy as a short-half-life adjunct; shockwave, dry needling and the physio's filter for injectable-adjacent claims.

**Sources:** [RTS tests and criteria after ACLR, scoping review 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0968016025002145) · [AOSSM RTP metrics, Winter 2025](https://www.sportsmed.org/membership/sports-medicine-update/winter-2025/return-to-play-after-acl-integrating-key-metrics) · [LSI overestimates knee function, JOSPT](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2017.7285) · [BFR vs general rehab after ACLR, KSSTA 2025](https://esskajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ksa.12527) · [Eccentric vs isometric vs HSR for patellar tendinopathy, network meta-analysis](https://www.cell.com/heliyon/fulltext/S2405-8440(24)15202-4) · [Hamstring rehab comparative effectiveness](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1360859225002682) · [ACWR systematic review & meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12487117/) · [FMS predictive validity](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7767371/) · [NSCA youth resistance training position statement](https://www.nsca.com/globalassets/about/position-statements/position_stand_youth_resistance_training---2009.pdf) · [IFSPT / World Physiotherapy Congress 2025](https://ijspt.scholasticahq.com/article/143626-the-international-federation-of-sports-physical-therapy-at-the-world-physiotherapy-congress-2025-shaping-a-global-commitment-to-sustainable-athlete-h) · [Kabaddi knee injury epidemiology](https://brieflands.com/journals/asjsm/articles/13314)

## 5. Hall C — Sports Science, Nutrition & Athlete Health

**Format:** 12-min talks, 4 per hour + chaired panel. `[C]` = has a formal consensus basis — cite the document on the slide (useful for the CME file). `[IN]` = India-specific angle.

**C1 · Energy availability & REDs** `[C: IOC 2023 REDs consensus]` — what actually changed from RED-S to REDs; applying the CAT2 traffic-light stratification across a 30-athlete squad; screening for low energy availability without a DXA in an Indian academy `[IN]`; male REDs — the missed diagnosis in wrestling, boxing and weightlifting `[IN]`.

**C2 · Nutrition periodisation on Indian diets** `[IN]` — fuel for the work required without inducing LEA; hitting 1.6–2.2 g/kg protein on a vegetarian diet (dal, dairy, the leucine threshold problem); building a pre-competition menu from rice, roti, poha and idli rather than translating a Western one; feeding the residential hostel athlete — what a SAI/Khelo India mess can and cannot deliver.

**C3 · Hydration & thermoregulation in Indian heat** `[C: ACSM 2023]` `[IN]` — WBGT is not air temperature: writing a heat policy a match referee will enforce; sweat rate and sodium at 33°C/85% RH and why imported prescriptions under-deliver; exertional heat stroke — cool first, transport second (cold-water immersion to <39°C before the ambulance moves); ten-day acclimatisation for travelling teams.

**C4 · Supplements & ergogenic aids** `[C: WADA strict liability]` — the five that survive scrutiny (creatine, caffeine, beta-alanine, nitrate, bicarbonate); contamination risk in the Indian supplement market and what batch certification does and doesn't cover `[IN]`; strict liability explained to an athlete; ayurvedic and herbal ergogenics — the evidence-to-marketing gap `[IN]`.

**C8 · Exercise physiology & performance testing** — durability, the endurance parameter your VO₂max test misses; critical power/speed as a field tool; lactate thresholds without a lab; force plates and the CMJ — which metrics track fatigue and which track noise.

**C9 · Wearables, AI & athlete monitoring** — only ~43% of wearable studies report adequate validity: a buyer's checklist before your federation signs; GPS and workload after a decade of criticism; why AI injury-prediction models keep failing external validation; **athlete data governance under India's DPDP Act** — consent, ownership and the federation contract `[IN]`.

**C12 · Concussion** `[C: Amsterdam 2022 / 6th International Consensus]` — SCAT6 and SCOAT6 in practice; the new visible signs mandating immediate removal (tonic posturing, impact seizure, ataxia, blank look); a Child SCAT6 protocol an Indian school can run without a physician on site `[IN]`.

**C13 · Anti-doping & WADA 2026 updates** `[C: 2026 Prohibited List, in force 1 Jan 2026]` — the 2026 List decoded for team clinicians (carbon monoxide as new M1.4, cell components, revised salmeterol intervals); building a TUE file that gets approved; **India top of WADA's ADRV table three years running** — 7,113 tests, 260 AAFs, 3.6% positivity, read honestly; where India's positives cluster (athletics 76, weightlifting 43, wrestling 29) and what support staff can change `[IN]`.
*Recommendation: record this block and publish it free — federation support staff who never register are exactly who needs it.*

**C14 · Sports cardiology & pre-participation screening** `[C: International Criteria; APSC recommendations]` — what's normal in a trained heart and the 2–7% abnormal-ECG downstream burden; APSC screening recommendations and their fit to South Asian athletes; screening at scale in Indian school sport — what's actually deliverable `[IN]`.

**C15 · Para-athlete health & injury surveillance** `[C: IOC 2020 surveillance consensus, STROBE-SIIS]` — thermoregulation in para-athletes (SCI, impaired sudomotor function, autonomic dysreflexia) in Indian heat `[IN]`; nutrition for the wheelchair athlete; what it would take to build a national injury registry for Indian sport `[IN]`.

**Reserve blocks:** C5 gut health & fuelling tolerance · C6 female athlete health & menstrual cycle `[C]` · C7 youth & academy athlete (bio-banding, age fraud, minimum-viable screening) `[IN]` · C10 recovery, sleep & travel · C11 sports psychology & mental health `[C: IOC 2019 + 2023]`.

## 6. What makes this programme distinctly Indian

A translated Western programme is the failure mode. These threads give SIC 2027 an identity:

- **Kabaddi** — Indian series data show ACL tears in 89.5% of injured players, 68.4% with meniscal involvement, 57.9% retiring from the sport, 16.6 months mean time lost. That single dataset earns a case in the ACL, meniscus, hip/groin *and* rehab sessions.
- **Cricket bowling workload** and the fast bowler's back — across surgical, rehab and load-management halls.
- **Heat and humidity** — heat policy, sweat sodium, acclimatisation. Non-negotiable for an Indian audience.
- **Vegetarian athlete nutrition** — protein targets and the leucine threshold on Indian diets.
- **Doping** — India has led WADA's violation table three years running. Confronting it directly is the most valuable thing Hall C can do.
- **Rehab without a lab** — most Indian clinics have no isokinetic dynamometer or force plate. Every rehab talk should state what to do without one.
- **Late presentation** — kabaddi ACL injuries present at a mean of ~14 months. Programme it as a clinical reality, not a footnote.

## 7. Decisions the Scientific Committee must take

1. **Two days or two + a pre-conference day?** The research yields ~3 days of strong content. A 28 Jan pre-conference day would hold the hands-on workshops, cadaver/dry-lab work and the reserve blocks. *This decision drives venue booking, so take it first.*
2. **Confirm the three-hall split** and approve hall titles.
3. **Faculty count.** Roughly **75–95 speaking slots** across three halls over two days, plus chairs. At 2 talks per faculty member that's ~40–50 faculty. Anchor 2–3 marquee names first and use them to recruit.
4. **Which debates run.** Two per hall per day is the ceiling — more and they stop feeling like events.
5. **Ethics/GCP session** — confirm it stays in the grid as a joint plenary. **TSMC's Declaration Proforma requires it** for CPD accreditation.
6. **Workshop ticketing.** Hands-on sessions must be capped and ticketed at registration, with per-station rosters. Uncapped "hands-on" becomes a lecture with mats on the floor.
7. **Equipment sponsorship boundary.** Force plates and dynamometers will be vendor-supplied — tie them to an exhibitor package, but keep teaching faculty independent of the vendor and say so from the podium. This is the biggest credibility risk in the rehab track.
8. **Recruit S&C faculty, not only physiotherapists**, for Hall B — an all-physio track loses the S&C audience by mid-morning.
9. **Free-paper prizes per track** so physiotherapy and nutrition PG entries don't compete against surgical case series.

## 8. Two research caveats

- The **ISAKOS/AOSSM/ESSKA** programme pages blocked direct fetching, so Hall A structures are reconstructed from search-indexed programme and abstract listings rather than read off the printed programme. Worth a manual check of the ESSKA 2026 highlight-lecture list before finalising.
- **IASMCON's session-level programme is not published in machine-readable form.** Hall C is built from consensus literature and Indian data rather than a competitor programme — a call to the IASM secretariat before finalising would de-risk duplication.
