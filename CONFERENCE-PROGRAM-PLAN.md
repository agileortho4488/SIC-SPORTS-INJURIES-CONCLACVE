# SIC 2027 — Two-Hall Scientific Program Plan

**Hyderabad Sports Injuries Conclave · 29th & 30th January 2027**
Draft for the Scientific Committee · prepared from a review of IASCON 2025/26, IASMCON 2025, ISKSAA, ISAKOS 2025, AOSSM 2025, ESSKA 2026, IFSPT/World Physiotherapy, NSCA, and the IOC/WADA/ACSM consensus literature. Every source is listed at the end of each hall section.

> **Structure finalised:** two halls, decided by the Chairman. Return to sport is the through-line. Sports science, nutrition and athlete health are **not** a third room — they sit inside Hall B, next to the people who act on them. This document was originally drafted as three halls; section 5 explains what that costs.
>
> **Hall A format finalised (this revision):** the Chairman's own design — a single clinical case run by three surgeons with differing views, then a physiotherapist, then a strength & conditioning coach, closing with a floor debate among all of them on how to actually manage that patient. Section 3 rebuilds Hall A around nine of these case blocks. Section 8 lists real, named, verified faculty candidates for outreach — sourced from society rosters and actual conference programmes, not invented.

## 1. The architecture — why two halls

One hall forces a compromise programme: surgeons sit through nutrition talks, physiotherapists sit through arthroscopic technique. Three halls solve that but split a first-year conclave too thin — a 20% room is a room that looks empty in photographs, and it puts nutrition and sports science in front of nutritionists only, which is precisely the audience that already agrees.

Two halls keep the split where the professions genuinely diverge — operative vs non-operative — and put the science in front of the clinicians who have to act on it.

| | Hall A | Hall B |
|---|---|---|
| **Title** | Surgery & Sports Orthopaedics | Rehab, Physiotherapy, S&C & Sports Science |
| **Audience** | Orthopaedic & arthroscopy surgeons, sports physicians, ortho PGs | Sports physiotherapists, S&C coaches, athletic trainers, nutritionists, exercise physiologists, team doctors, physio PGs |
| **Expected share** | ~45% | ~55% |
| **Room need** | Largest (also hosts plenary) | Large + floor space for hands-on |

**Sizing rule:** Hall A doubles as the plenary hall, so it must seat the full delegate count. Hall B now carries the larger share of delegates — size it at roughly 55–60% of Hall A, not the 40% a secondary room would need.

### Plenary sessions — everyone in one room

*(Called "plenary," not "joint" — this document is organised by anatomical joint throughout, so reusing that word for combined-hall sessions was its own source of confusion. Fixed here.)*

These are deliberately **not** parallel. They are the moments that make it one conclave:

| Session | When | Why joint |
|---|---|---|
| Inauguration | Day 1, 09:00 | Ceremonial; sets the theme |
| **Opening keynote** | Day 1, 09:15 | One big name, everyone hears it |
| **Return-to-Sport: The Case, Together** — the flagship session, run to the exact Hall A case-block structure (see box below) | Day 1, close of morning | The single decision every profession in the building shares. This is the signature session of SIC 2027 — everyone watches it happen once, together, before Hall A splits the same format into nine parallel cases. |
| **Medical ethics & good clinical practice** | Day 1, late afternoon | **Mandatory for TSMC CPD accreditation** — the Declaration Proforma requires it. Running it jointly guarantees attendance is captured. |
| Athlete fireside chat | Day 2, midday | Draws every audience; strong media moment |
| Awards & valedictory | Day 2, close | Best paper/poster across both tracks |

Everything else runs in parallel.

> **Return-to-Sport: The Case, Together — the exact running order.** This is the same template as every Hall A case block (Section 3), run once for the whole conclave before Hall A splits into parallel cases:
>
> A real, complex case with genuine disagreement built in -- e.g. a national-level athlete 7 months post-ACLR, cleared by imaging, failed on hop-test symmetry. Chair presents it (2 min). **Surgeon** -- was the surgery/graft choice right, would they have done anything differently in hindsight (8 min). **Physiotherapist** -- the criteria this athlete actually failed and why "7 months" was never the real clock (10 min). **S&C coach** -- what "return to performance" demands that "return to play" doesn't test for (8 min). **Nutritionist** -- 3 min, only if directly relevant to the case (e.g. an energy-availability angle). Then **12 minutes, floor open to all four plus the athlete themselves if present**: "she's in front of you right now, symmetric on paper, not right in the coach's eyes -- what do you actually tell her?" This is the session every delegate in the building watches at once, and it is the proof-of-concept for the nine case blocks that follow it in Hall A.

## 3. Hall A -- Case-Based Debate

**This is the Chairman's format, not a copy of anyone else's programme.** No published conference anywhere in the evidence base runs a surgeon-physio-S&C same-case debate -- the closest precedents are AOSSM's case-based Instructional Course (moderator presents a case to a 4-6 person panel, <=10-min talks, 15 min mandatory audience Q&A) and ISAKOS's "Battle" format (opposed short talks + moderated decision). SIC 2027 combines both and adds the S&C voice that neither one has. That is the meeting's actual point of difference -- sell it as such in the brochure.

**Block template (63 min, 9 blocks across two days):**

| Slot | Time | Who | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 min | Chair | Presents the real case -- age, sport, mechanism, findings. One patient, not an abstract topic. |
| 2 | 10 min | Surgeon 1 | Their surgical read of the case and why |
| 3 | 10 min | Surgeon 2 | A genuinely different surgical view -- different technique, different threshold, or "I wouldn't operate" |
| 4 | 8 min | Surgeon 3 | Third angle -- revision/salvage risk, a technical pearl, or the paediatric/adolescent variant of the same injury |
| 5 | 10 min | Physiotherapist | The rehab arc for whichever path is chosen -- criteria, not calendar dates |
| 6 | 8 min | S&C coach | Return-to-performance, not just return-to-play -- loading, testing, the training-age question |
| 7 | 15 min | **All five, floor open** | Chair puts the SAME case back to the panel: "he's in front of you now -- what do you actually do?" Audience polls before and after. |

Talks are capped at 10 min because every major society (AOSSM, ESSKA, ISAKOS) converges on that ceiling for exactly this reason: past 10 minutes a "talk" becomes a lecture and the panel loses its case-based feel. The 15-minute closing debate is **protected time** -- chairs must not let slot overruns eat into it; it is the reason delegates chose this block over the alternative track in Hall B.

**The nine case blocks:**

**CB1 - Recurrent shoulder instability in the thrower/bowler** *(flagship -- opens Day 1, 10:00)* -- 19-year-old fast bowler or throwing-sport athlete, recurrent anterior dislocation, glenoid bone loss on 3D-CT. Surgical debate: arthroscopic Bankart + remplissage vs Latarjet vs free bone block, and where the bone-loss threshold actually sits. Physio: return-to-throwing progression, not just return-to-sport. S&C: shoulder loading programme, scapular control, when to reintroduce full-velocity bowling. *This is the case the organisers described first -- the template every other block follows.*

**CB2 - ACL rupture in the pivoting athlete** *(Day 1, 11:20)* -- kabaddi or football player, complete ACL tear, meniscal involvement. Surgical debate: graft choice (hamstring vs quadriceps tendon vs BTB), repair-with-augmentation for the right 5%, lateral extra-articular tenodesis or not. Physio: criteria-based RTP, not a 9-month calendar date. S&C: the kabaddi-specific loading pattern -- India's own injury data (89.5% ACL tear rate in injured kabaddi players) makes this the most locally relevant case in the programme.

**CB4 - The torn meniscus -- save it or not** *(Day 1, 15:35)* -- posterior root tear with extrusion in a 30-year-old recreational athlete. Surgical debate: repair vs partial meniscectomy vs "meniscectomy is not a crime" -- the cases where resection is genuinely right. Physio: post-repair protected weight-bearing vs post-meniscectomy accelerated return. S&C: the load-management argument for protecting a repaired meniscus long-term.

**CB3 - Rotator cuff in the overhead athlete** *(Day 1, 14:30)* -- masked subscapularis tear in a 45-year-old badminton/tennis player. Surgical debate: repair vs superior capsular reconstruction vs lower trapezius transfer, fatty infiltration as the deciding factor. Physio: the non-operative pathway and when it's failing. S&C: scapular mechanics and the load that caused it in the first place.

**CB5 - Hamstring tear & reconditioning** *(Day 2, 08:30)* -- grade 2b hamstring tear in a sprinter/footballer, first vs second occurrence. Surgical/sports-physician debate: conservative vs surgical repair thresholds, MRI's real value for "Monday's decision." Physio: L-protocol vs conventional rehab timelines, lengthened-state training. S&C: sprint reintroduction dosing and the reinjury-risk argument for not rushing return-to-sprint.

**CB6 - The unstable ankle & stress fractures around it** *(Day 2, 09:35)* -- chronic lateral ankle instability presenting alongside a missed navicular stress fracture. Surgical debate: modified Broestrom vs augmented reconstruction, and when a stress fracture changes the surgical plan entirely. Physio: proprioceptive rehab vs bracing long-term. S&C: load progression back to cutting sports, why the stress fracture happened.

**CB7 - Hip & groin pain in the athlete** *(Day 2, 10:55)* -- footballer or kabaddi player with FAI and adductor-related groin pain, failed a physiotherapy trial. Surgical debate: arthroscopy threshold after physio failure, labral repair vs reconstruction, borderline dysplasia -- scope or PAO. Physio: the groin-pain clinical entity classification and where physio genuinely changes the outcome. S&C: the loading programme that should have been tried before referral.

**CB8 - Fast bowler's lumbar stress fracture** *(cricket marquee -- Day 2, 14:10, immediately post-lunch)* -- 17-year-old academy fast bowler, contralateral pars stress reaction on MRI. Sports-physician/surgeon debate: conservative rest-and-graduated-return vs the rare surgical indication, imaging thresholds for return. Physio: the graduated bowling-load return protocol. S&C: bowling workload monitoring -- the metric that actually predicts this injury vs the ones that don't. *This is the single most India-relevant case in the programme -- a Hyderabad audience includes BCCI-adjacent and Telangana cricket clinicians. Programme it to be recorded and clipped for the brochure.*

**CB9 - Hand & wrist fractures in sport** *(Day 2, 15:20, closing block)* -- cricketer or kabaddi player, scaphoid fracture from a fall, plus a brief second case (boxer's fracture / finger dislocation) as a rapid-fire coda. Surgical debate: cast vs percutaneous fixation vs ORIF thresholds, playing-through-injury with protective casting. Physio: early mobilisation vs immobilisation. S&C: grip/loading return and the decision on whether the athlete can compete before full union.

**Reserve blocks** *(pre-conference day, if approved):* CB10 Elbow in the overhead athlete (UCL, OCD of the capitellum); CB11 Revision & failed surgery -- the case that came back.

## 4. Hall B — Rehabilitation, Physiotherapy, S&C & Return to Play

**Format:** 12–15 min talks; blocks close with a moderated discussion rather than per-talk Q&A. Two ticketed, capped hands-on workshops.

**B1 · Criteria-based rehab & the RTP decision** — "time is not a criterion"; what proportion of athletes would actually pass a full battery at 9 months post-ACLR; return to participation → sport → performance; psychological readiness (ACL-RSI) and the athlete whose knee passes but whose head doesn't; who owns the decision and how it's documented. Closes by building a one-page RTP criteria sheet live from the floor.
**Debate:** *"No athlete should be cleared for pivoting sport before 9 months post-ACLR."*

**B2 · Objective testing, screening & technology** — force plates in a small clinic: the five CMJ metrics worth reporting and the twenty that aren't; handheld dynamometry done properly (belt fixation, normalisation, MDC); isokinetics vs field testing in 2027; movement screening after FMS; markerless motion capture — good enough to change a decision?; **panel:** *"What would you buy first with ₹5 lakh?"*

**B3 · Tendinopathy & load-driven pain** — tendon pain as a loading problem; isometrics for analgesia (where "45 seconds × 5" came from and when it fails); heavy slow resistance vs eccentrics for patellar tendinopathy; insertional vs mid-portion Achilles; gluteal tendinopathy — compression is the enemy; energy storage and release, the phase everyone skips.

**W1 · Hands-on carousel** (90 min, three 28-min stations, ~30 per station):
1. **Objective testing bench** — live CMJ/drop jump on volunteers, reading force–time curves on screen; dynamometry with vs without belt fixation (showing the tester-strength artefact); writing the actual report line a surgeon and a coach will each read.
2. **Tendon loading lab** — Spanish squat rigged with a belt and pole (no proprietary kit); HSR tempo work; modifying every exercise for a clinic with one bench, one step, one barbell; the 24-hour pain-response rule on case vignettes.
3. **Plyometric progression** — pogo → hop → bound ladder with landing-quality criteria; take a 3-month post-ACLR athlete to month 7 with only cones, a box and floor space.

**W2 · Field-based RTP testing & hamstring reconditioning** (Day 2, 90 min) — build a 40-minute RTP battery with no lab (505, hop series, Askling H-test, sprint exposure); hamstring L-protocol and Nordic technique coached hands-on; sprint reintroduction dosing; the pass/fail sheet you hand the coach.

**B5 · Muscle injury & the hamstring** — classifying injury (BAMIC, MLG-R) and whether MRI changes Monday; L-protocol vs conventional (28 vs 51 days) and reinjury; lengthened-state training and adherence reality; calf and adductor — the two you're under-rehabbing; return to running → return to sprinting.

**B6 · Load management & injury risk** — what happened to ACWR (from "predicts injury" to a cluster RCT in 482 elite youth footballers showing no difference); metrics that survive a real Indian club season; bowling workload and the fast bowler's back; sleep, energy availability and travel — the load that never appears in the GPS file.

**B7 · The athlete who isn't a 25-year-old male** — youth resistance training is protective (and how to say that to a parent); apophyseal injury — load modification, not rest; the female athlete and menstrual cycle: what low-quality evidence permits you to claim; REDs as a modifiable rehab variable; para-athlete rehab and why your normative data doesn't apply.

**B4 · Adjuncts under scrutiny** *(reserve)* — BFR: what 2024–25 meta-analyses actually show (comparable, not superior) and the three groups where the cuff earns its place; manual therapy as a short-half-life adjunct; shockwave, dry needling and the physio's filter for injectable-adjacent claims.

## 5. Hall B, science stream — Sports Science, Nutrition & Athlete Health

**This is not a separate hall.** These blocks run inside Hall B, and the strongest of them (concussion, sports cardiology, anti-doping) are promoted to plenary session so the surgical audience gets them too — which is the point of collapsing the third room rather than deleting it. The `C` numbering is kept only so the committee can match sessions against the earlier three-hall draft.

**Format:** 12-min talks, 4 per hour + chaired panel. `[C]` = has a formal consensus basis — cite the document on the slide (useful for the CME file). `[IN]` = India-specific angle.

**C1 · Energy availability & REDs** `[C: IOC 2023 REDs consensus]` — what actually changed from RED-S to REDs; applying the CAT2 traffic-light stratification across a 30-athlete squad; screening for low energy availability without a DXA in an Indian academy `[IN]`; male REDs — the missed diagnosis in wrestling, boxing and weightlifting `[IN]`.

**C2 · Nutrition periodisation on Indian diets** `[IN]` — fuel for the work required without inducing LEA; hitting 1.6–2.2 g/kg protein on a vegetarian diet (dal, dairy, the leucine threshold problem); building a pre-competition menu from rice, roti, poha and idli rather than translating a Western one; feeding the residential hostel athlete — what a SAI/Khelo India mess can and cannot deliver.

**C3 · Hydration & thermoregulation in Indian heat** `[C: ACSM 2023]` `[IN]` — WBGT is not air temperature: writing a heat policy a match referee will enforce; sweat rate and sodium at 33°C/85% RH and why imported prescriptions under-deliver; exertional heat stroke — cool first, transport second (cold-water immersion to <39°C before the ambulance moves); ten-day acclimatisation for travelling teams.

**C4 · Supplements & ergogenic aids** `[C: WADA strict liability]` — the five that survive scrutiny (creatine, caffeine, beta-alanine, nitrate, bicarbonate); contamination risk in the Indian supplement market and what batch certification does and doesn't cover `[IN]`; strict liability explained to an athlete; ayurvedic and herbal ergogenics — the evidence-to-marketing gap `[IN]`.

**C8 · Exercise physiology & performance testing** — durability, the endurance parameter your VO₂max test misses; critical power/speed as a field tool; lactate thresholds without a lab; force plates and the CMJ — which metrics track fatigue and which track noise.

**C9 · Wearables, AI & athlete monitoring** — only ~43% of wearable studies report adequate validity: a buyer's checklist before your federation signs; GPS and workload after a decade of criticism; why AI injury-prediction models keep failing external validation; **athlete data governance under India's DPDP Act** — consent, ownership and the federation contract `[IN]`.

**C12 · Concussion** `[C: Amsterdam 2022 / 6th International Consensus]` — SCAT6 and SCOAT6 in practice; the new visible signs mandating immediate removal (tonic posturing, impact seizure, ataxia, blank look); a Child SCAT6 protocol an Indian school can run without a physician on site `[IN]`.

**C13 · Anti-doping & WADA 2026 updates** `[C: 2026 Prohibited List, in force 1 Jan 2026]` — the 2026 List decoded for team clinicians (carbon monoxide as new M1.4, cell components, revised salmeterol intervals); building a TUE file that gets approved; **India top of WADA's ADRV table three years running** — 7,113 tests, 260 AAFs, 3.6% positivity, read honestly; where India's positives cluster (athletics 76, weightlifting 43, wrestling 29) and what support staff can change `[IN]`.
*Recommendation: record this block and publish it free — federation support staff who never register are exactly who needs it.*

**C14 · Sports cardiology & pre-participation screening** `[C: International Criteria; APSC recommendations]` — what's normal in a trained heart and the 2–7% abnormal-ECG downstream burden; APSC screening recommendations and their fit to South Asian athletes; screening at scale in Indian school sport — what's actually deliverable `[IN]`.

## 6. What makes this programme distinctly Indian

A translated Western programme is the failure mode. These threads give SIC 2027 an identity:

- **Kabaddi** — Indian series data show ACL tears in 89.5% of injured players, 68.4% with meniscal involvement, 57.9% retiring from the sport, 16.6 months mean time lost. That single dataset earns a case in the ACL, meniscus, hip/groin *and* rehab sessions.
- **Cricket bowling workload** and the fast bowler's back — across surgical, rehab and load-management halls.
- **Heat and humidity** — heat policy, sweat sodium, acclimatisation. Non-negotiable for an Indian audience.
- **Vegetarian athlete nutrition** — protein targets and the leucine threshold on Indian diets.
- **Doping** — India has led WADA's violation table three years running. Confronting it directly is the most valuable thing the science stream can do — and as a plenary session it now reaches the surgeons too.
- **Rehab without a lab** — most Indian clinics have no isokinetic dynamometer or force plate. Every rehab talk should state what to do without one.
- **Late presentation** — kabaddi ACL injuries present at a mean of ~14 months. Programme it as a clinical reality, not a footnote.

## 7. Decisions the Scientific Committee must take

1. **Two days or two + a pre-conference day?** The research yields ~3 days of strong content. A 28 Jan pre-conference day would hold the hands-on workshops, cadaver/dry-lab work and the reserve blocks. *This decision drives venue booking, so take it first.*
2. **Hall titles.** The two-hall split is settled; confirm the wording of the titles, particularly whether Hall B leads with "Rehab" or "Return to Sport".
3. **Faculty count.** Hall A's 9 case blocks need 3 surgeons + 1 physio + 1 S&C coach each = **45 Hall-A seats** (fewer with deliberate reuse — a strong CB1 surgeon can also sit on CB3's panel, and Dr. Debasis Chatterjee could plausibly fill both CB6 and CB9), plus Hall B's ~50–65 lecture slots. Section 8 has at least one named candidate for every block; the honest remaining gap is a fast-bowler-stress-fracture clinician for CB8, and second/third surgical opinions for CB5–CB7/CB9 beyond the first name given.
4. **Which debates run.** Two per hall per day is the ceiling — more and they stop feeling like events.
5. **Ethics/GCP session** — confirm it stays in the grid as a plenary session. **TSMC's Declaration Proforma requires it** for CPD accreditation.
6. **Workshop ticketing.** Hands-on sessions must be capped and ticketed at registration, with per-station rosters. Uncapped "hands-on" becomes a lecture with mats on the floor.
7. **Equipment sponsorship boundary.** Force plates and dynamometers will be vendor-supplied — tie them to an exhibitor package, but keep teaching faculty independent of the vendor and say so from the podium. This is the biggest credibility risk in the rehab track.
8. **Recruit S&C and nutrition faculty, not only physiotherapists**, for Hall B — it now carries the science stream as well, and an all-physio programme loses both the S&C and the nutrition audience by mid-morning.
9. **Free-paper prizes per track** so physiotherapy and nutrition PG entries don't compete against surgical case series. With two halls, keep three prize categories (surgical / rehab / science) even though there are only two rooms.
10. **Which four reserve blocks return.** See the note in section 2 — C3 heat and C4 supplements are the strongest candidates if a pre-conference day is approved.
11. **Faculty outreach sequencing.** CB1 (shoulder instability) is fully staffed with verified names and should be the first block confirmed — a confirmed CB1 surgeon becomes the strongest recruiting tool for everyone else. Physiotherapy and S&C now have real candidates for every block (Section 8), mostly current IPL franchise medical/conditioning staff. **One seat remains genuinely open after two research passes: a surgeon or sports physician for CB8 (fast bowler's lumbar stress fracture)** — this needs a personal introduction through the committee's own network, not further cold search. Do not publish any name on the public site or brochure until that person has personally confirmed — see the site's existing "faculty announcement to follow" convention.
