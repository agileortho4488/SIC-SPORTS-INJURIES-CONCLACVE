01 · THE ARCHITECTURE

ONE THEME, TWO HALLS

Return to sport is the theme of SIC 2027. Every session answers one question from a different angle — how do we get this athlete back, safely and at full performance? Two parallel halls give the surgical and the rehabilitation audiences a full two days of depth each, while sports science and nutrition are woven into the sessions where they belong rather than separated into a track of their own. Joint plenary sessions keep it one conclave.

HALL ASurgery & Sports OrthopaedicsOrthopaedic & arthroscopy surgeons, sports physicians, PG trainees · ~55% of delegates
HALL BRehabilitation, Physiotherapy & Strength and ConditioningSports physiotherapists, S&C coaches, athletic trainers, sports scientists · ~45%

Room sizing: Hall A doubles as the plenary hall and must seat the full delegate count. Hall B needs roughly half that, plus clear floor space for the hands-on workshops.

03 · HALL A

SURGERY & SPORTS ORTHOPAEDICS

Format: 12-minute talks (10 speak + 2 changeover), hard stops enforced by chairs. Debates 2 × 8 min + rebuttal, with audience polling before and after. Pre-record all "live" surgery — the operating surgeon narrates the recording live. This gives the teaching value without OT-link risk. Chair pairs, one senior + one mid-career who runs the clock.

A1 · Instability: beyond the Bankart

Measuring bone loss that changes the plan (3D-CT vs glenoid track vs MRI) · Bankart + remplissage into subcritical bone loss · Latarjet 2027: classic vs congruent-arc, screw vs button, open vs arthroscopic · free bone block after failed Latarjet · posterior and multidirectional instability in throwers and gymnasts · relive: arthroscopic Latarjet — the six steps where it goes wrong.

DEBATE · 19-year-old kabaddi player, first dislocation — stabilise now vs rehab and wait
A2 · Rotator cuff: repair, augment, transfer

Massive but reparable — margin convergence, interval slides, knowing when to stop pulling · SCR vs lower trapezius transfer, with fatty infiltration as the discriminator · patch and bioinductive augmentation: what 2025 evidence supports and what it doesn't · the subscapularis tear you're still missing · case panel: three cuffs, three plans.

A4 · ACL I — the primary reconstruction debates

Graft selection 2027: is quadriceps tendon now the default? (international survey — QT preferred for 61% of primaries, 89% of revisions) · hamstring sizing and fixation when the graft measures 7 mm · ACL repair with internal bracing: which 5% of tears? · ramp lesions and why they're missed at index surgery · relive: QT ACLR + modified Lemaire LET, narrated end to end.

DEBATE · Every high-risk primary ACL needs a lateral extra-articular tenodesis
A5 · ACL II — the complex & multiligament knee

Posterior tibial slope: when to measure, when to cut — slope-reducing osteotomy in the re-ruptured knee · the multiligament knee in India: road-traffic vs kabaddi patterns, single-stage vs staged · PCL and the residual sag nobody reports · posterolateral corner reconstruction that fits a 90-minute list · RTS criteria with no isokinetic dynamometer.

A6 · Save the meniscus

Posterior root tears — fixation angle, tension, and why extrusion persists · meniscal centralisation: promising or over-sold? · radial and horizontal cleavage tears in the young adult · "meniscectomy is not a crime" — the 15% where resection is right · allograft and scaffold pathways in India · relive + panel: root and ramp repair in the same knee, including the complication.

A7 · Cartilage & joint preservation

The 2 cm² decision: microfracture vs AMIC vs minced vs MACI, with Indian cost reality · single-stage minced cartilage and the 2025 matched-pair data · osteochondral auto- and allograft, and the supply problem nobody discusses · unloading osteotomy is treatment · RTS after cartilage surgery — 9 months is a floor, not a target · meet the expert: audience-submitted hardest case.

A8 · Hip & groin

Groin pain algorithm for footballers and kabaddi players that survives contact · FAI syndrome after the physiotherapy trials — who still gets a scope? · labral repair vs reconstruction vs augmentation, and does the capsule have to be closed? · how I do it: hip arthroscopy setup, portals, avoiding cam under-resection.

DEBATE · Borderline dysplasia (LCEA 20–25°) — arthroscopy + plication vs PAO
A9 · Foot & ankle in sport

Chronic lateral instability decision tree: arthroscopic Broström, InternalBrace, or anatomic reconstruction · osteochondral lesions of the talus — size thresholds · Achilles in the athlete · syndesmosis: button vs screw and how much malreduction matters · posterior impingement and FHL in dancers.

A10 · The paediatric & adolescent athlete

ACL in the skeletally immature — transphyseal vs all-epiphyseal vs IT-band (growth disturbance <2%, re-tear 9–15%) · tibial spine and patellar sleeve avulsions · adolescent patellar instability: MPFL alone, or must you address trochlea and TT-TG? · OCD of knee and capitellum · the overtrained 14-year-old and the academy culture problem.

A11 · Orthobiologics: evidence vs marketing

What ESSKA-ORBIT supports (PRP in KL 1–3 knee OA, lateral epicondylitis) and what it does not · BMAC, adipose and "stem cell" clinics against placebo-controlled data, plus the Indian regulatory position · consent, cost and honest claims with an Indian patient.

DEBATE · Biologics have no place in the arthroscopic OR in 2027
A12 · When it goes wrong — revision & failed surgery RESERVE

A workup that finds the real cause of the failed ACL (tunnel, slope, meniscus, alignment, rehab) · one- vs two-stage revision when autograft is spent · recurrent instability after Bankart or Latarjet · septic arthritis after arthroscopy — retain or remove the graft? · panel: "My worst complication" — four faculty, unedited. Programme this last on Day 2 — it is the session people stay for.

04 · HALL B

REHABILITATION, PHYSIOTHERAPY & STRENGTH AND CONDITIONING

Format: 12–15 minute talks; blocks close with a moderated discussion rather than per-talk Q&A. Two ticketed, capped hands-on workshops — these are the reason physiotherapists pay to attend rather than watch online. Recruit S&C faculty, not only physiotherapists: an all-physio track loses the S&C audience by mid-morning of Day 1. Sports-science and nutrition content lives in this hall — see the two dedicated blocks below.

B1 · Criteria-based rehabilitation & the RTP decision

"Time is not a criterion" — what criteria-based actually means when you write the discharge note · what proportion of your athletes would truly pass a full test battery at 9 months post-ACLR · return to participation → sport → performance · psychological readiness (ACL-RSI) and the athlete whose knee passes but whose head doesn't · who owns the decision, and how it is documented. Closes by building a one-page RTP criteria sheet live from the floor.

DEBATE · No athlete should be cleared for pivoting sport before 9 months post-ACLR
B2 · Objective testing, screening & technology

Force plates in a 400-sq-ft clinic: the five countermovement-jump metrics worth reporting and the twenty that aren't · handheld dynamometry done properly — belt fixation, normalisation, minimum detectable change · isokinetics vs field testing in 2027 · movement screening after FMS: what survived the validity literature · markerless motion capture — good enough to change a clinical decision? · panel: "What would you buy first with ₹5 lakh?"

B3 · Tendinopathy & load-driven pain

Tendon pain as a loading problem, in one slide · isometrics for analgesia: where "45 seconds × 5" came from and how often it fails · heavy slow resistance vs eccentrics for patellar tendinopathy — the network meta-analysis verdict · insertional vs mid-portion Achilles: why the same programme fails one · gluteal tendinopathy — compression is the enemy, stretching is the trap · energy storage and release, the phase everyone skips.

W1 · Hands-on carousel TICKETED · CAPPED ~30/STATION

Station 1 — Objective testing bench: live CMJ and drop jump on volunteers, reading the force–time curve on screen; dynamometry with vs without belt fixation, showing the tester-strength artefact; writing the actual report line a surgeon and a coach will each read.
Station 2 — Tendon loading lab: Spanish squat rigged with a belt and pole, no proprietary kit; HSR tempo work; modifying every exercise for a clinic with one bench, one step and one barbell; the 24-hour pain-response rule on case vignettes.
Station 3 — Plyometric progression: pogo → hop → bound ladder with landing-quality criteria; take a 3-month post-ACLR athlete to month 7 using only cones, a box and floor space.

W2 · Field-based RTP testing & hamstring reconditioning HANDS-ON

Build a 40-minute RTP battery with no lab — 505 change of direction, hop series with a tape measure, Askling H-test, sprint exposure with timing gates, and honest caveats on phone-app timing · hamstring L-protocol and Nordic technique coached hands-on, with regressions · sprint reintroduction dosing and the criterion for the first max-effort sprint · the pass/fail sheet you hand the coach.

B5 · Muscle injury & the hamstring

Classifying muscle injury (BAMIC, MLG-R) — and whether the MRI changes what you do on Monday · L-protocol vs conventional strengthening: 28 days vs 51 days, and what newer reviews say about reinjury · lengthened-state training and the Nordic: fascicle length vs adherence reality · calf and adductor — the two you are under-rehabbing · from return to running to return to sprinting.

B6 · Load management & injury risk

What happened to ACWR — from "predicts injury" to a cluster RCT in 482 elite youth footballers showing no difference in injury rates · metrics that survive a real Indian club season: sRPE, weekly change, chronic capacity, low-tech monitoring that actually gets collected · bowling workload and the fast bowler's back · sleep, energy availability and travel — the load that never appears in the GPS file.

B7 · The athlete who isn't a 25-year-old male

Youth resistance training is protective — the position-statement evidence, and how to say it to a parent and a coach · the maturing athlete: apophyseal injury, load modification not rest · the female athlete and the menstrual cycle: what genuinely low-quality evidence permits you to claim · REDs as a modifiable rehabilitation variable · para-athlete rehabilitation and why your normative data doesn't apply.

B4 · Adjuncts under scrutiny RESERVE

Blood flow restriction: what 2024–25 meta-analyses actually show (comparable, not superior) and the three groups where the cuff earns its place · manual therapy as a short-half-life adjunct — using it without building dependence · shockwave, dry needling, and the physiotherapist's filter for injectable-adjacent claims.

Fuelling the return — nutrition, energy availability & recovery DAY 2

Nutrition for injury recovery: protein targets and the leucine threshold on a vegetarian Indian diet · energy availability and REDs as a modifiable rehabilitation variable — screening without a DXA · hydration, sweat sodium and heat in Indian training conditions · the five supplements that survive scrutiny, plus contamination risk and strict liability · sleep and recovery as the load that never appears in the GPS file.

The mind of the returning athlete DAY 2

Psychological readiness: ACL-RSI and I-PRRS as gate criteria, not paperwork · fear-avoidance — identifying the athlete who is physically cleared but will not compete · the conversation that resets an athlete’s expectations · disordered eating in aesthetic and weight-category sport: what the physiotherapist, the coach and the dietitian each own.

06 · WHAT MAKES THIS PROGRAMME INDIAN

THE THREADS THAT MAKE IT OURS

A translated Western programme is the failure mode of a new conference. These threads run through all three halls and give SIC 2027 an identity no international meeting can copy.

  • Kabaddi. Indian series data show ACL tears in 89.5% of injured players, 68.4% with meniscal involvement, 57.9% never returning to the sport, and 16.6 months mean time lost among those who do. That single dataset earns a case in the ACL, meniscus, hip/groin and rehabilitation sessions.
  • Cricket bowling workload and the fast bowler's back — across surgical, rehabilitation and load-management halls.
  • Heat and humidity. Heat policy, sweat sodium, acclimatisation. Non-negotiable for an Indian audience and absent from most imported programmes.
  • Vegetarian athlete nutrition — protein targets and the leucine threshold on Indian diets.
  • Doping. India has led WADA's violation table three years running. A reserve block on the 2026 Prohibited List for team clinicians is the most valuable single hour the conclave could add.
  • Rehabilitation without a lab. Most Indian clinics have no isokinetic dynamometer or force plate. Every rehab talk should state what to do without one.
  • Late presentation. Kabaddi ACL injuries present at a mean of ~14 months. Programme it as a clinical reality, not a footnote.

07 · DECISIONS FOR THE COMMITTEE

WHAT WE NEED SIGNED OFF

1 · Two days, or two plus a pre-conference day?

The research yields roughly three days of strong content. A 28 January pre-conference day would hold the hands-on workshops, any dry-lab work and the reserve blocks. Take this decision first — it determines the venue contract.

2 · Confirm the two-hall split and approve the hall titles.

Hall A doubles as the plenary hall and must seat the full delegate count. Sports science and nutrition sit inside Hall B rather than in a hall of their own.

3 · Faculty scale.

Roughly 55–70 speaking slots across two halls over two days, plus chairs — about 30–38 faculty at two talks each. For an inaugural edition, lock 2–3 marquee anchors first and recruit everyone else off their names. Confirm in writing 6–9 months out and keep a reserve bench.

4 · Which debates run.

Two per hall per day is the ceiling. More and they stop feeling like events.

5 · The ethics / GCP session stays as a joint plenary.

TSMC's Declaration Proforma requires it for CPD accreditation. Non-negotiable.

6 · Workshop ticketing.

Hands-on sessions must be capped and ticketed at registration with per-station rosters. Uncapped "hands-on" becomes a lecture with mats on the floor.

7 · Equipment sponsorship boundary.

Force plates and dynamometers will be vendor-supplied — tie them to an exhibitor package, but keep teaching faculty independent of the vendor and say so from the podium. This is the single biggest credibility risk in the rehabilitation track.

8 · Free-paper prizes per track.

So physiotherapy and nutrition entries don't compete against surgical case series.

Two research caveats, stated plainly. The ISAKOS, AOSSM and ESSKA programme pages blocked direct retrieval, so Hall A structure is reconstructed from search-indexed programme and abstract listings rather than read off the printed programme — worth checking the ESSKA 2026 highlight-lecture list before finalising. And IASMCON does not publish a session-level programme in machine-readable form, so the sports-science content now folded into Hall B is built from the consensus literature and Indian data rather than a competitor programme.
